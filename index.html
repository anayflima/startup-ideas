The idea in one line. A venture-funded company of companies. We build products inside one company, one at a time, each aiming for millions (then tens of millions) in ARR, all running on one shared AI company machine for GTM, onboarding, support and ops. We keep the companies and we don't sell the machine.

Why now

  • Big, horizontal TAMs are exactly where the AI labs go next (a lab already shipped a Granola-type notes app). Focused markets are below their radar.
  • AI made building cheap. The bottleneck is now running a company: distribution, onboarding, support, ops. That is what the machine does.
  • We are funded, so we have runway to get each product right, one at a time, with no hires for now: just the two founders plus the machine. Cash also lets us buy companies sooner.

Proof it can work

  • Rippling builds many products in parallel on one data layer: about $570M ARR (2025).
  • Bending Spoons runs many brands on one shared stack: about $1.31B revenue (2025).
  • Zoho runs 55+ apps on one platform: about $1.4B revenue.
  • Investors back the model: Thrive Holdings ($3B+), General Catalyst Creation ($1.5B).

Company of companies

One company that builds, owns and runs several products on a shared AI machine.

What it is

Each product has its own brand. For now it is just the two founders, no hires: we build one product at a time, together with the company machine: the shared AI system that launches and runs every product. Humans do the 0 to 1 (pick the market, talk to customers, shape the product); the machine does the repeatable work. When a product shows product-market fit, it gets more of our time and money, and once the machine runs it, we start the next one. Later, with cash flow, we also buy stagnant software companies and move them onto the machine (the Bending Spoons step).

How it differs from what exists

ModelWhat they doOur difference
Bootstrapped holding (Tiny, saas.group, Constellation)Buy profitable small software, grow slowly from cash flowWe build first, with venture money, so we move faster and can buy later
Venture studio (Atomic, Hexa)Launch startups, then spin each out with its own founders and cap tableEverything stays inside one company and one cap table, on the same machine
Compound startup (Rippling)Many products for the same buyer on one data layerSame buyer or different markets; the shared asset is the operating machine, not only the data
AI platform (The Company Company)Sell agents-run-your-company software to other companiesWe don't sell the platform; we make money from the companies it runs

Pros

  • Several shots on goal over time; we learn fast what works.
  • Lean: no hires for now, just the two founders plus the machine, so burn stays low and runway is long.
  • The machine is a compounding asset and a real moat.
  • Focused markets avoid the labs and big incumbents.
  • Fits us: ML, finance and PE exposure, and strong engineering and product.
  • Investors already fund this shape (Rippling, Bending Spoons, Thrive Holdings).
  • Later M&A engine: stagnant software sells for about 3.5 to 5.5x ARR.

Cons and risks

  • Each product still needs its own PMF and its own channel.
  • Focus can split; lempire and Zoho grew one hit first, then added products.
  • The story to investors must add up to a venture outcome, not a set of lifestyle businesses.
  • Speed: one product at a time is slower, and soft kill rules can waste months.
  • Many brands, legal entities and support queues add overhead.
  • AI makes building cheap for everyone, so focused markets fill up faster too.

Open questions

  • Same buyer (cross-sell, like Rippling or lempire) or unrelated markets (portfolio, like Bending Spoons)?
  • One brand or many brands under one parent?
  • When do we start hiring, and for what?
  • When does M&A start: from day one, or after the first products hit about $1M ARR?
  • How do we report it to investors: total ARR, cash flow, or per-product metrics?

Playbook (draft)

How a new product would go from idea to running on the machine.

Idea filter: a market passes when

  • B2B buyers already pay for software or services in this area.
  • There is a channel we can reach: a community, a marketplace, SEO, a clear outbound list.
  • Too focused for the labs to care, but big enough for $5M to $20M ARR.
  • Testable in 4 to 6 weeks with the machine.
  • Ideally fits our backgrounds or shares a buyer with another product.

Stages and gates

  1. Explore (2 to 4 weeks): 20+ customer conversations, a landing page from the machine, a waitlist or letters of intent.
  2. Build (4 to 6 weeks): MVP on shared auth, billing and onboarding; first paying users.
  3. Prove (about 90 days): kill if there are no paying customers or no MRR growth; keep the learnings in the machine.
  4. Scale: steady monthly MRR growth and good retention earn more of our time and budget.
  5. Run: the machine runs GTM, support and ops; humans move to the next product.

The company machine

  • GTM machine: landing page, LinkedIn page, daily posts, outbound, ads.
  • Auto onboarding and product tours.
  • Self-healing support and ops agents.
  • Shared auth, billing, analytics and one dashboard across products.
  • A launch template, so every product starts from the same base.

Capital rules (our assumptions)

  • Fixed budget per product per stage; money follows the gates, not opinions.
  • No hires for now: spend goes to tools, compute and GTM tests, not salaries.
  • One product at a time; the next one starts when the machine runs the current one or it is killed.
  • Buying companies later uses a hurdle rate, like Constellation (about 20 to 25% IRR).

Planning assumptions, not researched numbers.

AWS for agent automations

Cloud infrastructure that runs AI agents and automations for you.

The idea

Nobody needs a Mac mini running OpenClaw 24/7. Set up every day do X, Y, Z online; it runs on our servers and the results come to you.

Already exists

  • Hosted OpenClaw: OpenClaw Cloud, V2 Cloud, HoopyClaw, or a cheap VPS.
  • Developer infra: Modal, E2B, Trigger.dev.
  • No-code agents: Lindy, Zapier Agents, Manus, n8n Cloud, Relevance AI.
  • Big labs: ChatGPT Scheduled Tasks and Agent Mode, Claude Managed Agents.

Pros

  • Real pain and recurring revenue.
  • Fits our ML and infra background.
  • Growing market; can start narrow.

Cons

  • Big labs are already there.
  • Crowded and generic without a wedge.
  • Capital and ops heavy; thin margins.
  • Trust and security of logins on our servers.

Where it fits

Not a standalone product for now. Most useful as the internal infrastructure of the company machine.

The Company Company

thecompany.company: an AI layer that runs a business, sold to other companies.

What they do

  • Tagline: The AI that runs your business. A team plus a company of always-on agents, with shared memory and all your tools, working 24/7.
  • The company is shown as departments (Engineering, Product and Design, Go-to-Market, Operations) with named agents working next to humans.
  • Agents take tasks like reading 212 replies to a welcome email, writing an onboarding spec, closing the books, refunding a double charge or shipping a checkout; humans review and approve.
  • Sold as SaaS: Individual and Teams plans (Pro, Max, Ultra).

Why it matters to us

  • A funded team bets that agents can run every department.
  • They sell the shovels; we mine. They make money from the platform, we make money from the companies the platform runs.
  • We could use tools like this inside our machine instead of building every piece.
  • Risk: if our machine gets very good, selling it is tempting, and that puts us back in their market.

Voice notes and thinking

Where the ideas came from.

14 Nov · big TAM ideas

Big-TAM ideas are exactly what the labs go after (a lab shipped something that replaces a Granola-type app). Maybe pick smaller TAMs that are not attractive enough for them.

14 Nov · the Bending Spoons idea

Bending Spoons buys companies that stopped growing, keeps the users, rebuilds with far fewer people and raises revenue. Our version: a company factory used internally, not sold. We do the 0 to 1, then AI runs GTM, users and sales. Products can have different names but belong to one company.

14 Nov · how it would work

Develop several ideas in parallel and make everything reproducible: a GTM machine, self-healing systems, an auto LinkedIn page and daily posts, quick onboarding. Put human time where PMF shows up. Later, with money, buy companies and make them efficient.

14 Nov · The Company Company

They sell this layer to companies to make them more efficient. We would build it internally to launch our own companies and make money from them.

15 Nov · funded, not bootstrapped

Many companies make millions in ARR; several of them together add up to a lot. We are venture-funded, so this is not bootstrapping: a big company of companies, with the products built inside, scaled with one playbook.

15 Nov · one at a time, no hires

No running several bets in parallel and no platform team. We are not hiring anyone for now: the two of us build one product at a time with the machine.
