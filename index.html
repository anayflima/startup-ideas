Company of companies

Build many products inside one company and run them all on a shared AI machine.

A venture-funded company of companies. We build several products inside one company, each aiming for millions and then tens of millions in ARR. A shared AI company machine runs GTM, onboarding, support and ops for all of them. We keep the companies and don't sell the machine.
$570MRippling ARR, built as many products in parallel
$1.31BBending Spoons revenue on one shared stack
55+apps Zoho runs on one platform
$3B+raised by Thrive Holdings for AI-run portfolios

How it works

1

Pick a focused market that pays for software and is too small for the AI labs to care about.

2

A small pod does the 0 to 1: customer calls, product shape, first users.

3

The machine runs it: landing page, LinkedIn, outbound, onboarding, support, ops.

4

Double down or kill: PMF earns people and money, no traction gets shut down fast.

Why now

  • Big horizontal TAMs are where the labs go next (a lab already shipped a Granola-type notes app). Focused markets stay below their radar.
  • AI made building cheap. Running a company is now the bottleneck, and that is what the machine does.
  • We're funded, so we can run several bets in parallel and hire a platform team early.

Later: buy companies

With cash flow, buy stagnant software companies (they sell for about 3.5 to 5.5x ARR) and move them onto the machine, like Bending Spoons does with Evernote, WeTransfer and Vimeo.

Why it could work

  • Many shots on goal; one failed product doesn't kill the company.
  • The machine compounds: every launch makes the next one cheaper and faster.
  • Focused markets avoid head-on fights with the labs.
  • Investors already fund this shape (Rippling, Bending Spoons, Thrive Holdings).

What could go wrong

  • Distribution is the hard part. The machine speeds up execution, not finding demand.
  • Focus: several half-built products instead of one great one.
  • The portfolio must add up to a venture-size outcome ($100M+ ARR).
  • Burn grows fast if the kill rules are soft.

Open questions

  • Same buyer for every product (like Rippling) or unrelated markets (like Bending Spoons)?
  • One brand or many brands under one parent?
  • How many pods at once: 3, 5 or 8?
  • When do acquisitions start?

Next steps

  • Pick 3 to 5 focused B2B markets to test (filter in Playbook).
  • Decide the first machine pieces: GTM machine, auto onboarding, self-healing support.
  • Talk to 2 or 3 multi-product operators (lempire, saas.group, Bending Spoons alumni).

Funded companies that already build or run many products.

Rippling

~$570M ARR

"Compound startup": HR, IT and finance products built in parallel on one data layer. $1.8B+ raised, $16.8B valuation (2025).

Takeaway: building many products at once can be a venture story.

Bending Spoons

~$1.31B revenue

Buys mature brands (Evernote, WeTransfer, Vimeo, Eventbrite), moves them to a shared stack, cuts teams and raises prices. About $2.7B of M&A in 2024-25, funded by $710M equity and $2.8B debt.

Takeaway: the shared machine is the asset; debt can fund acquisitions.

Thrive Holdings

$3B+ raised

AI roll-up spun out of Thrive Capital: buys services firms (accounting, IT) and embeds engineers to automate them. Raised $2B in Aug 2026 at $12B.

Takeaway: investors pay for AI-run portfolios.

General Catalyst Creation

$1.5B fund

Incubates AI platforms that buy services businesses and automate 30 to 70% of the work.

Takeaway: the same thesis, from the investor side.

Atlassian, HubSpot, Zoho

Zoho ~$1.4B

Launch new products inside (Atlassian's Point A program) on shared identity, billing and UI, then cross-sell. Zoho runs 55+ apps with no VC.

Takeaway: how to launch products inside one company.

Studios: Atomic, Hexa, Forum

Atomic IV $320M

Build startups in house, then spin each one out with its own founders and cap table.

Takeaway: the launch process. The difference: we keep the companies.

Rules of thumb

  • SaaS at $1-10M ARR usually runs at 20-40% profit.
  • Small SaaS sells for about 3.5-5.5x ARR (or 3-5x profit).
  • With AI running GTM and support, a product could need only 1 to 2 people.

Example

5 products at $2M ARR each = $10M ARR, about $2-4M profit a year and worth roughly $35-55M. At venture scale the goal is 10+ products, or a few that reach $20M+ each.

A draft of how a new product goes from idea to running on the machine.

A market passes when

  • B2B buyers already pay for software or services in it.
  • There's a reachable channel: a community, marketplace, SEO or a clear outbound list.
  • Too focused for the labs, big enough for $5-20M ARR.
  • Testable in 4 to 6 weeks.
  • Fits our backgrounds or shares a buyer with another product.

The company machine

  • GTM: landing page, LinkedIn page, daily posts, outbound, ads.
  • Auto onboarding and product tours.
  • Self-healing support and ops agents.
  • Shared auth, billing, analytics and one dashboard.
  • A launch template for every new product.

Stages and gates

1

Explore, 2-4 weeks: 20+ customer calls, a landing page, a waitlist or letters of intent.

2

Build, 4-6 weeks: MVP on shared auth, billing and onboarding; first paying users.

3

Prove, ~90 days: no paying customers or no MRR growth means kill it and keep the learnings.

4

Scale: steady MRR growth and good retention earn a bigger pod.

5

Run: the machine runs it; humans move to the next product.

Capital rules (our assumptions)

  • Fixed budget per pod per stage; money follows the gates.
  • At about $10M a year of spend: platform team $3-4M, 6 to 8 pods at $0.5-0.8M each.
  • Acquisitions use a hurdle rate, like Constellation (~20-25% IRR).

Planning assumptions, not researched numbers.

thecompany.company sells an AI layer that runs a business. It's the closest product to our machine.

What they do

  • "The AI that runs your business": a team plus always-on agents with shared memory and all your tools.
  • The company is drawn as departments (Engineering, Product, GTM, Operations) with named agents next to humans.
  • Agents read 212 replies to a welcome email, write specs, close the books, refund charges and ship code; humans approve.
  • Sold as SaaS: Individual and Teams plans (Pro, Max, Ultra).

What it means for us

  • A funded team is betting agents can run every department.
  • They sell the shovels; we mine. They earn from the platform, we earn from the companies it runs.
  • We could use tools like this inside our machine instead of building every piece.
  • Risk: if our machine gets very good, selling it is tempting, which puts us in their market.

AWS for agent automations

Cloud infrastructure that runs AI agents and automations for you.

Nobody should need a Mac mini running OpenClaw 24/7. Set up "every day do X, Y, Z" online; it runs on our servers and the results come to you.

Why it could work

  • Real pain and recurring revenue.
  • Fits our ML and infra background.
  • Growing market; can start narrow, e.g. one-click hosted OpenClaw.

What could go wrong

  • The big labs are already there.
  • Crowded and generic without a wedge.
  • Capital and ops heavy, with thin margins between LLM costs and cheap VPSs.
  • Trust and security of logins on our servers.

Where it fits

Hard to win as a standalone horizontal product. It works best as a narrow vertical (one persona, ready-made automations, Swiss/EU hosting) or as the infrastructure inside the company of companies machine.
